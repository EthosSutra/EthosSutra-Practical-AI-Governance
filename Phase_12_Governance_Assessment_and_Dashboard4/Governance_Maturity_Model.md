# Executive Summary

Enterprise AI Governance is not implemented in a single project or achieved through compliance with one regulation.

It evolves through the progressive development of governance capabilities, operational maturity, organizational accountability and continual improvement.

The Enterprise AI Governance Capability Maturity Model (AIGCMM) provides a structured framework for evaluating the maturity of AI Governance capabilities across GlobalBank Corporation.

Unlike traditional maturity models that measure documentation or policy existence, this model evaluates the operational effectiveness of governance capabilities through evidence, performance, implementation consistency and executive oversight.

The maturity model enables organizations to understand their current governance state, define future governance objectives and establish measurable improvement roadmaps aligned with business strategy, regulatory obligations and industry best practices.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Enterprise AI Governance Capability Maturity Model enables GlobalBank Corporation to:

- Measure governance capability maturity.
- Benchmark governance performance across business units.
- Identify governance improvement opportunities.
- Prioritize governance investments.
- Support executive decision-making.
- Demonstrate continual improvement.
- Prepare for certification and regulatory reviews.

# 2. Maturity Model Philosophy

Enterprise AI Governance maturity is determined by the effectiveness of governance capabilities rather than the existence of governance documentation.

A mature governance program demonstrates:

- Clearly defined governance structures.
- Operationally effective processes.
- Consistent implementation.
- Objective evidence.
- Measurable performance.
- Continual improvement.

Governance maturity therefore reflects organizational capability rather than administrative completeness.

# 5. Enterprise Maturity Levels

| Level | Maturity Stage | Description |
|--------|----------------|-------------|
| Level 1 | Initial | Governance activities are informal, reactive and largely dependent on individual effort. |
| Level 2 | Developing | Basic governance processes exist but implementation remains inconsistent across the organization. |
| Level 3 | Defined | Governance processes are documented, approved and consistently implemented. |
| Level 4 | Managed | Governance effectiveness is measured using KPIs, monitoring and periodic management review. |
| Level 5 | Optimized | Governance is continually improved through automation, analytics, benchmarking and executive governance leadership. |

These maturity levels apply consistently across every governance capability evaluated within the Enterprise AI Governance Framework.

# 8. Current-State vs Target-State Assessment

The maturity model evaluates governance from two complementary perspectives.

| Assessment Perspective | Purpose |
|------------------------|---------|
| Current-State Maturity | Measures the organization's existing governance capability. |
| Target-State Maturity | Defines the desired governance capability aligned with strategic objectives and regulatory expectations. |
| Gap Analysis | Identifies capability gaps requiring remediation and investment. |
| Improvement Roadmap | Defines prioritized actions to achieve the target maturity state. |

This approach ensures that maturity assessments drive measurable governance improvement rather than static scoring exercises.

# 10. Capability Maturity Characteristics

The following table illustrates how governance capabilities evolve across maturity levels.

| Maturity Level | Observable Characteristics |
|----------------|----------------------------|
| **Initial** | Governance activities are informal, undocumented and reactive. Success depends on individual knowledge rather than organizational processes. |
| **Developing** | Basic governance processes exist but implementation is inconsistent across teams. Limited documentation and evidence are available. |
| **Defined** | Governance processes are standardized, documented and consistently executed. Roles and responsibilities are clearly understood. |
| **Managed** | Governance effectiveness is measured through KPIs, dashboards, audits and periodic management reviews. Corrective actions are tracked and verified. |
| **Optimized** | Governance is continuously improved using automation, analytics, benchmarking, lessons learned and executive strategic planning. |
