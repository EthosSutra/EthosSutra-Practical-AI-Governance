# Executive Summary

The Enterprise AI Governance Assessment Workbook is the operational execution engine of the Enterprise AI Governance Assessment Methodology.

It provides a structured, repeatable and evidence-driven mechanism for evaluating governance capabilities, measuring implementation maturity, identifying governance gaps and producing executive-ready reporting.

Unlike traditional audit checklists that focus primarily on documentation, the workbook evaluates governance implementation through standardized assessment questions, objective evidence, maturity scoring, control validation and regulatory traceability.

The workbook transforms governance evidence into measurable governance performance, enabling GlobalBank Corporation to continuously assess and improve the governance maturity of GlobalBank Nexus.

# 9. Assessment Scoring Methodology

The Enterprise AI Governance Assessment Workbook applies a standardized scoring methodology to ensure assessment results remain objective, repeatable and comparable across governance capabilities, business units and assessment periods.

Each governance capability is evaluated using the six assessment dimensions defined within the Enterprise AI Governance Assessment Methodology.

| Assessment Dimension | Weight | Evaluation Objective |
|----------------------|-------:|----------------------|
| Governance | 20% | Accountability, ownership and oversight |
| Process | 15% | Documented and repeatable operational processes |
| Technology | 15% | Supporting tools, automation and technical controls |
| Evidence | 20% | Availability and quality of objective evidence |
| Performance | 15% | KPIs, monitoring and governance effectiveness |
| Compliance | 15% | Alignment with internal policies, standards and regulations |

The weighted assessment score determines the maturity of each governance capability and contributes to the overall Enterprise AI Governance Maturity Score.

# 18. Enterprise Best Practices

| Best Practice | Business Value |
|--------------|----------------|
| Assess governance capabilities rather than individual documents | Measures operational effectiveness rather than documentation completeness |
| Collect evidence continuously instead of only before assessments | Improves audit readiness and reduces assessment effort |
| Standardize assessment questions and scoring | Increases consistency across assessors and business units |
| Validate findings independently before executive reporting | Strengthens confidence in assessment conclusions |
| Use dashboards and heatmaps to communicate results | Enables faster executive decision-making |
| Reassess governance capabilities periodically | Supports continual improvement and governance maturity |

---

# 19. Lessons Learned

| Observation | Recommendation |
|------------|----------------|
| Strong documentation alone does not demonstrate effective governance | Evaluate operational implementation and supporting evidence |
| Different assessors may interpret findings differently | Apply standardized scoring and evidence requirements |
| Manual evidence collection increases assessment effort | Automate evidence collection wherever feasible |
| Governance maturity changes over time | Perform periodic reassessments and trend analysis |
| Executive reporting should focus on business outcomes rather than technical detail | Present findings using dashboards, KPIs and prioritized actions |
