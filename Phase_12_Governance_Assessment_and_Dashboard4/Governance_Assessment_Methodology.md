# Executive Summary

An Enterprise AI Governance Assessment evaluates the effectiveness, maturity and operational implementation of an organization's AI Governance Program.

Unlike traditional compliance reviews that focus primarily on documentation, this methodology evaluates governance capabilities across people, processes, technology, controls, operational execution and objective evidence.

The assessment determines whether governance activities are consistently implemented, effectively managed, supported by verifiable evidence and aligned with internationally recognized frameworks including the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF), ISO/IEC 42001:2023 and the European Union AI Act.

The methodology provides a structured, repeatable and evidence-based approach that enables GlobalBank Corporation to measure governance maturity, identify implementation gaps, prioritize remediation activities and provide executive assurance regarding the governance of GlobalBank Nexus.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Enterprise AI Governance Assessment Methodology establishes a standardized approach for evaluating the design, implementation, effectiveness and continual improvement of AI Governance across the organization.

The methodology enables GlobalBank Corporation to:

- Measure governance maturity.
- Evaluate governance effectiveness.
- Validate implementation of governance controls.
- Verify operational compliance.
- Assess alignment with international standards.
- Identify governance gaps and improvement opportunities.
- Support internal audits and regulatory inspections.
- Provide objective executive assurance.

---

# 2. Assessment Principles

The methodology is based on the following principles:

- Governance capability is assessed rather than documentation alone.
- Conclusions shall be supported by objective evidence.
- Assessments shall remain independent, repeatable and risk-based.
- Governance effectiveness shall be measured across the complete AI lifecycle.
- Findings shall focus on continual improvement rather than fault identification.
- Assessment results shall support executive decision-making and strategic governance planning.

---

# 3. Assessment Philosophy

The objective of an AI Governance Assessment is not to determine whether documentation exists.

The objective is to determine whether governance capabilities are consistently implemented, operationally effective, objectively evidenced and continually improving.

Every governance conclusion must therefore be supported through demonstrable implementation rather than policy statements alone.

# 6. Governance Capability Assessment Model

Rather than assessing isolated controls, the methodology evaluates governance capabilities.

Each capability represents a collection of policies, processes, controls, technologies and operational practices working together to achieve a governance objective.

Examples include:

- AI Risk Management
- Human Oversight
- AI Lifecycle Governance
- Vendor Governance
- AI Monitoring
- Audit & Assurance

Each capability is assessed independently and then aggregated into the overall governance maturity profile.

# 7. Assessment Dimensions

Every governance capability is evaluated across six standardized dimensions.

| Dimension | Assessment Objective |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Governance | Roles, accountability and oversight are clearly established. |
| Process | Processes are documented, approved and consistently followed. |
| Technology | Supporting technology and automation operate effectively. |
| Evidence | Implementation is supported by objective and verifiable evidence. |
| Performance | Governance effectiveness is measured using defined KPIs and metrics. |
| Compliance | Activities satisfy applicable regulatory and framework obligations. |

A governance capability is considered effective only when all dimensions operate together.

# 9. Evidence Maturity Model

The quality of an AI Governance Assessment depends on the quality of evidence collected.

Not all evidence provides the same level of assurance. This methodology classifies evidence according to its maturity and reliability.

| Level | Evidence Type | Examples | Assurance Level |
|--------|---------------|----------|-----------------|
| Level 1 | Interview Evidence | Stakeholder interviews, verbal explanations | Low |
| Level 2 | Documentary Evidence | Policies, procedures, standards, meeting minutes | Moderate |
| Level 3 | Operational Evidence | Completed forms, approvals, workflow records, training logs | High |
| Level 4 | Technical Evidence | System configurations, screenshots, audit logs, monitoring dashboards | Very High |
| Level 5 | Automated Evidence | Continuous monitoring dashboards, automated control validation, system-generated compliance metrics | Highest |

Higher maturity evidence provides stronger assurance and reduces dependence on subjective interpretation.

---

# 10. Evidence Confidence Model

Assessment conclusions shall consider both the quantity and quality of supporting evidence.

| Confidence Rating | Assessment Criteria |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| High | Multiple independent evidence sources consistently support the assessment conclusion. |
| Medium | Evidence exists but is limited in scope or consistency. |
| Low | Evidence is incomplete, inconsistent or primarily based on interviews. |

Assessment reports should clearly indicate confidence levels for significant governance findings.

---

# 11. Governance Effectiveness Rating

Governance effectiveness is evaluated using a six-level maturity scale.

| Rating | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Optimized | Governance is continuously improved using performance metrics, automation and executive oversight. |
| Managed | Governance is consistently implemented, measured and periodically reviewed. |
| Defined | Governance processes are documented and consistently applied across the organization. |
| Developing | Governance processes exist but are inconsistently implemented or lack supporting evidence. |
| Initial | Governance activities are informal, reactive or partially documented. |
| Absent | Governance capability has not been established. |

This rating reflects operational effectiveness rather than documentation completeness.

# 13. Finding Classification Methodology

Assessment findings are classified according to business impact and governance significance.

| Rating | Description | Typical Response |
|---------|-------------|------------------|
| Critical | Immediate risk to legal compliance, security or business operations. | Immediate remediation and executive escalation. |
| High | Significant governance weakness requiring urgent corrective action. | Corrective action within defined timelines. |
| Medium | Governance improvement required with moderate operational impact. | Planned remediation and management monitoring. |
| Low | Minor enhancement opportunity with limited risk exposure. | Continuous improvement activity. |
| Observation | Recommendation to strengthen governance maturity. | Consider during future governance planning. |
